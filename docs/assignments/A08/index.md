# A8 – Gears

## Setup
This week we were tasked with making a gear box to house a gear and a pinion. The appropriate part numbers for all the pieces according to McMaster: Pinion(7880K19), Gear(7880K24), Input journal bearing(6391k111), Output journal bearing(6391k131), Dowel pins(98381A471), Nut(90480A005), Screw(92314A115). 

## Input Shaft
The input shaft transmits power from the gear to the pinion. It is 1 in long and 0.125 in in diameter. My hand calculations gave a minimum value of 0.084 in which proves that the shaft is sufficient with a diameter of 0.125 in.

## Output Shaft
The output shaft carries the gear and is the larger of the two shafts with a diameter of 0.25 in. The shaft is sufficient because it is larger than the calculated value of 0.091 in. 

<img width="427" height="640" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/f7375a0c-189d-4136-8cfb-5a1dc9cf7658" />




## Journal Bearings
The bearings were made of oil-embedded 841 bronze because according to the tables it passes all three limits. I sized them from the gear tooth load, which I solved for using the ideal gear train formula which equals 2.50 lbf, leading to a total tooth force of 2.66 lbf. I assigned that whole F value to a single bearing. I chose a bearing length of 0.25 in for both shafts.

## Sketch
The housing is a two-part encapsulating object, a cover and a base, that bisects the plan perpendicular to the shafts. This split was convenient since the sleeve bearings had to be pressed into the pockets, which couldn't be achieved any other way. The fasten method I decided on was to utilize 4 #4-40 nuts and bolts. On the bottom side of the base there is a pre cut hole for the nuts in order to fully secure the gearbox.
